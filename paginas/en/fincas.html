Two farms to build the first model.

San Juan de Frías and San Juan de Potosí, in Colombia's Magdalena Medio.

The assets

We start by transforming our own farms

Each farm will have its own design, budget and tracking. We want to recover the productive potential of paddocks overgrown with brush and weeds and with room for better management, through infrastructure, pastures and precision ranching. We will document the starting point and how it evolves.

How we operate

Precision ranching in operating modules

We will implement high-density rotations supported by water, fencing, observation and records. Modules let us organize teams, budgets, herds and tracking.

4 modules

At Frías, of approximately 233 hectares each.

1 unit

Potosí, modeled as a single 389-hectare unit.

~250 ha

The operating unit we want to develop: repeatable and adaptable to each property.

The final design of each module responds to its topography, its water and its production capacity.

The sequence

Transformation takes investment and order

Each investment enables a specific capability. Before the cattle arrive, forage, water and management must be in place. Animals come in according to the capacity actually available.

01

Diagnose and design each property.

02

Prepare pastures, fencing, water and handling infrastructure.

03

Train the team and bring in herds as the enabled capacity allows.

04

Measure production, costs and commercial performance to adjust the operation.

What can scale

Our farms will be the first demonstration of the model

We will record the transformation and its results so we can learn and improve. Once there is enough evidence, we will open the farms for visits and learning to landowners, producers, partners and investors.

If we validate the model, the next stage would be to operate third-party farms: the owner keeps the land and San Juan provides the management and operating service.
