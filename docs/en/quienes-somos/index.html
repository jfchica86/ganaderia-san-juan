Ganadería San Juan

Who we are and what we believe

Honor our roots. Transform our future.

We believe the best way to honor what we have received is to dare to build something even better.

Ganadería San Juan was born from a family story, from a deep connection to the land, and from a question that stays with us: what could be possible if we learned to understand and manage our land in a fundamentally different way?

Our father spent many years of his life acquiring, working and caring for the farms we are fortunate to inherit today. He left us far more than land and cattle. He left us a story of hard work, a family legacy and an extraordinary opportunity.

We want to honor that legacy.

But we don’t believe honoring a tradition means repeating the same practices forever. We believe it means keeping the best of what has been learned, recognizing the work of those who came before us, and using the knowledge and possibilities of our time to keep building.

We don’t start from the idea that traditional ranching is wrong about everything. We start from curiosity: what can we understand better, what can we do differently, and how much additional value can we create?

Today we know more about photosynthesis, soil biology, animal nutrition and how ecosystems work. We have tools to observe, measure and analyze things that used to be hard to know. And we have technologies that can extraordinarily expand our capacity to learn and make decisions.

And yet the fundamental principles have not changed. Plants still need light, water and nutrients. Animals still depend on the quality of their feed and on their well-being. Soil is still the foundation of our productive capacity.

We don’t want to reinvent nature. We want to learn to work better with it.

Our ambition is to build a cattle company that can produce abundantly, generate profit, improve the condition of its land, protect biodiversity and create extraordinary opportunities for people.

A company where scientific knowledge lives alongside field experience. Where technology is not just a faster way of doing the same thing, but a tool for discovering different ways to produce and create value.

And where people don’t stay on the land for lack of alternatives, but can choose it because they find a future there that is exciting, prosperous and full of possibilities.

We are starting on our family farms, San Juan de Frías and San Juan de Potosí, in Cimitarra, Santander. At Frías we also have Yuwú, a 218-hectare forest whose protection is part of our vision.

We are still at the beginning of the transformation we imagine. We have the land, the experience, the convictions and the determination. We are looking for the partners, the knowledge and the capital needed to make it real.

We don’t have all the answers. We have questions we consider important, and a commitment to find the answers by working, experimenting, measuring and learning.

We want to show that a cattle operation that is more productive, profitable, regenerative and humane is not only desirable: it can become an extraordinary company.

What we believe

Our convictions are the starting point. The results will be the proof.

We don’t want our principles to be nice phrases on a web page. We want them to guide the decisions we make, the investments we make, the people we bring on, and the way we measure our progress.

These are the principles with which we want to build Ganadería San Juan.

01. We are sun harvesters

Every blade of grass is a solar panel. And our job is to learn to harvest its energy without destroying its ability to keep capturing it.

Ranching begins long before there are any cattle. It begins when a plant receives sunlight and, through photosynthesis, turns that energy into plant matter.

Let’s imagine for a moment that every blade of grass is a small solar panel. The more healthy, active leaves a pasture has, the greater its potential to capture energy and turn it into biomass.

Now let’s imagine that, when grazing, we remove practically all of those leaves. It’s like taking the solar panels off a power plant and expecting it to keep producing electricity at the same rate.

The plant can regrow using its reserves, but if we repeatedly subject it to grazing that is too intense, we reduce its ability to recover, develop roots and produce new feed.

That is why our goal is not simply for the cattle to eat all the available grass. It is to learn to harvest part of that energy, keeping enough leaf area for the plant to continue growing.

And here something extraordinary appears: leaves don’t only produce feed for our animals. Through photosynthesis, plants capture carbon from the atmosphere and build it into their tissues. Part of that carbon reaches the soil through the roots, their exudates and plant residues, feeding fundamental biological processes.

A well-managed pasture can help keep the soil covered, encourage root development, improve the water cycle and, under certain conditions, increase the carbon stored in the soil.

In this way, the same activity that sustains our cattle production can help improve the conditions that make it possible to keep producing.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We want to learn to manage our pastures as true solar-energy capture systems.

This means understanding how fast the grass grows, how much leaf area it needs to keep, when it is ready to be grazed and how long it needs to recover.

We want our herders to look at a pasture and see more than just food for cattle. We want them to recognize millions of small solar panels at work, roots growing beneath their feet, and a living system whose productivity depends on the decisions we make.

We will measure biomass production, ground cover, pasture recovery and how our soils evolve.

Our aspiration is to produce more and more feed per hectare, use it efficiently and, at the same time, strengthen the land’s productive capacity.

Because our business doesn’t begin with cattle. It begins with the sun. And our first responsibility is to keep running the solar panels that turn it into life.

02. The grass decides, not the calendar

We believe nature keeps its own time, and our task is to learn to read it.

A plant doesn’t grow because thirty days have passed. It grows according to the availability of water, light, nutrients, temperature and the conditions around it.

Grazing too early can compromise its recovery. Grazing too late can reduce the quality of the feed available.

That is why a rotation shouldn’t depend on a calendar alone.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We want to design grazing systems where the animals’ movements respond to the real condition of the pastures.

We will use observation, height measurements, forage availability and plant-recovery criteria to decide when to enter and when to leave each paddock.

Rest periods will vary when conditions call for it.

Our goal is to develop a system where the team learns to read the grass and not simply to follow a schedule.

We want our decisions to follow biology, not to try to force biology to follow our decisions.

03. Density is a tool, not a miracle

We believe in the potential of high-density rotational grazing, but we don’t believe in magic solutions.

Concentrating animals for short periods can help us make better use of forage, spread manure and organize pasture management.

However, increasing the instantaneous density of animals doesn’t mean a farm can support more cattle indefinitely.

Productive capacity depends on how much feed grows, how much can be consumed and how much needs to stay so that plants and soil keep their ability to recover.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We want to develop fencing, subdivisions, water supply and planned movements that let us manage cattle with precision.

We will calculate the sustainable stocking rate from the feed supply, and we will pay special attention to periods of slower growth.

Our aspiration is to progressively increase productive capacity per hectare, but only to the extent that the system proves it can sustain it.

We don’t want more animals just because. We want more productivity because we learned to grow and use feed better.

04. Soil is the asset; beef is the dividend

We believe a farm’s true productive wealth lies in the capacity of its soil to sustain life.

Soil is not simply the surface animals walk on.

It is a complex system of minerals, water, air, roots, microorganisms and organic matter. Its condition shapes its ability to absorb and store water, cycle nutrients and sustain plant growth.

When we degrade the soil, we compromise our future capacity to produce.

When we improve how it functions, we can strengthen the foundations of long-term productivity.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We want to work with ground cover, living roots, proper grazing management, erosion protection and practices that encourage the soil’s biological activity.

We will set baseline measurements and follow up periodically to understand how it evolves.

We are interested in evaluating indicators such as cover, compaction, infiltration, organic matter and soil carbon, using methods suited to each measurement.

We don’t want to claim that we regenerate our land simply because we adopted certain practices.

We want to prove it by observing verifiable changes in the condition and functioning of the soil.

05. Water goes to the cattle, not the cattle to the water

We believe water should be a fundamental piece of infrastructure in the production system, not a difficulty the animals have to solve on their own.

Access to water affects intake, animal well-being, how grazing is distributed and the ability to run a farm efficiently.

When animals have to travel long distances to drink, or get direct access to streams and springs, it can lead to lost productivity and damage to water sources.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We want to design water supply, storage and distribution networks that bring water to the animals.

We will seek to protect streams, springs and sensitive areas, reducing cattle’s direct access where necessary.

The infrastructure will have to take into account topography, water availability and dry periods.

Our goal is for every productive division to have reliable, safe access, according to the needs of the system.

Water should let us manage the cattle better and, at the same time, take care of the land.

06. First principles before habit

We believe the best question is not always how something has been done, but why it works.

We deeply respect the knowledge built by generations of ranchers. Many traditional practices hold valuable observations about animals, plants, climate and land.

But we also believe no practice should be placed beyond questioning and improvement.

We want to learn to reason from the fundamentals: physics, chemistry, biology, animal behavior and economics.

How we want to do it at San Juan

Before adopting a practice, a technology or an investment, we want to understand what problem it solves and why it should work.

We will ask what the factors are that truly limit productivity, what assumptions we are making and what evidence we need to make a decision.

We will listen to those who know the land, to researchers, technicians, workers and people with different experiences.

We don’t want to replace tradition with new fads.

We want to combine accumulated experience with the freedom to question, experiment and discover better ways of doing things.

07. What we don’t measure, we can hardly improve

We believe good intentions are no substitute for results.

Ranching is full of variables: rainfall, grass growth, animal weight, costs, prices, fertility, health and many more.

If we don’t understand how those variables change, it is hard to know whether our decisions are creating value.

Measuring doesn’t mean filling the farm with gadgets or producing reports no one uses. It means getting the information we need to learn and decide better.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We want to build an operation where we can know productivity per hectare, weight gain, stocking rate, production costs, pasture yields and economic results.

We will also measure indicators related to soil, water, animal well-being and the development of our people.

We aspire to incorporate individual animal identification, digital tools, remote monitoring and data analysis when their usefulness justifies it.

We will distinguish proven results from estimates and hypotheses.

Information is only worth something when it helps us make better decisions.

08. We want to be herders, not just cowboys

We believe the future of ranching depends as much on the quality of its people as on the quality of its land.

We want to recover and expand what it means to be a herder.

A herder understands plants, watches the animals, recognizes changes in the environment and knows that their decisions affect how the whole system works.

We don’t want work on a farm to be reduced to moving cattle, fixing fences and following instructions.

We want people to develop judgment, autonomy and knowledge.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We aspire to build an organization where everyone has the chance to learn, study, experiment and take part in improving the operation.

We want to develop people who can interpret information, propose solutions, use technological tools and take on ever greater responsibility.

We will seek to build decent working conditions, competitive pay and real opportunities for growth, supported by a financially sound company.

We want asking questions to be welcome, differences to be discussed with respect, and honest mistakes to be a chance to learn.

The farm should grow because its people grow. And its people should prosper because together they build a better company.

09. Animal welfare is part of productivity

We believe treating animals well is not a concession to profitability. It is a condition for building a good cattle operation.

Animals respond to their environment, to handling, to the availability of feed and water, to heat and to stressful situations.

A company that understands those relationships can make better decisions to care for its animals and improve their performance.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We want to develop calm-handling protocols, planned movements and practices that reduce unnecessary stress.

We will design facilities that make it safe for people to work and make it possible to handle cattle properly.

We will pay attention to access to water, shade, nutrition, health and animal behavior.

We will measure indicators that let us identify problems and opportunities for improvement.

We want productivity to be the result of understanding and caring for animals better, not of demanding more from them than they can give.

10. Trees are productive infrastructure

We believe trees, pastures, water, animals and forests are all part of one territory.

For a long time, raising cattle and conserving trees have been presented as opposing activities.

We want to explore systems where trees contribute shade, animal well-being, soil protection, water regulation and biodiversity, without losing sight of the pastures’ needs.

We also believe natural forests have a value that should not be reduced to their possible economic use.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We want to evaluate and adopt silvopastoral practices, native trees, living fences and ecological connectivity corridors where conditions allow.

We will seek to protect sensitive areas and to understand how to better integrate the productive and natural components of the landscape.

At San Juan de Frías, Yuwú represents a special responsibility: to conserve 218 hectares of forest and explore ways to secure its protection over the long term.

We want to show that a profitable cattle operation can coexist with a deliberate decision to conserve.

We don’t see nature as an obstacle to producing. We want to learn to produce as part of it.

11. We prefer an uncomfortable truth to a pretty promise

We believe trust is built by telling the truth, especially when the results are not what we expected.

Farming and ranching carry uncertainties we cannot eliminate: climate variability, biological behavior, markets, costs and execution challenges.

We don’t want to build a company that hides those risks behind grand declarations.

We want to build a company that learns to recognize them, measure them and manage them.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We will separate our aspirations from the results we actually achieve.

When a practice doesn’t work, we want to understand why. When an investment doesn’t deliver the expected return, we want to acknowledge it and correct course.

We will share clear information about productivity, costs, risks and progress with our partners and investors.

And we will avoid claiming environmental benefits we have not been able to verify.

We want our credibility to depend on the quality of our facts, not the force of our words.

12. First experiment. Then prove. Finally scale.

We believe great transformations are built through small, well-designed tests and accumulated learning.

An idea can look extraordinary in a presentation and not work on a farm.

That is why we want to develop the discipline of turning ideas into hypotheses, hypotheses into experiments and experiments into useful knowledge.

Not everything new will be better. Not everything that works in one paddock will work the same in another.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We will begin by establishing baselines and designing pilot units that let us evaluate practices, infrastructure and technologies.

We will define what we expect to improve, how much it will cost to test it, what risks we are taking and how we will know whether it worked.

We will evaluate results under different conditions, including rainy and dry periods, before expanding major investments.

Initiatives that prove their value can become repeatable practices and, eventually, adaptable to other properties.

We don’t want to grow on promises. We want to grow on proven capabilities.

13. Technology must change what we make possible

We believe the most valuable technology is not necessarily the most sophisticated, but the one that transforms our ability to create value.

We don’t want to install devices just to say we are a modern cattle operation.

Nor do we want to digitize inefficient processes just to keep doing the same thing, only faster.

We want to explore the frontiers of technology and innovation without losing sight of the biological and economic foundations of what we do.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We want to evaluate artificial intelligence tools, computer vision, sensors, animal identification, image analysis, drones and predictive models.

We are interested in their potential to better understand pasture growth, estimate weights, spot problems early, optimize decisions and cut repetitive tasks.

But we also want to experiment with business models, financing structures and new sources of income.

Innovation can be found in an algorithm, in a fence, in a contract or in a completely different way of organizing the work.

We don’t want technology to look different. We want to think differently to discover which technologies we really need.

14. A cattle company can transform lives

We believe companies are vehicles for people to develop their abilities, build well-being and make their purposes real.

We want Ganadería San Juan to be a profitable business. Without profitability we cannot sustain our aspirations.

But we believe economic success takes on a deeper meaning when it makes it possible to create opportunities for others.

We imagine a farm where the work is intellectually stimulating, where learning is part of everyday life and where people can feel proud of what they build.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We want to develop a culture of continuous learning, responsible autonomy, open conversation and collaboration.

We aspire to offer good pay, training opportunities and spaces where people can propose, try things out and improve them.

We are interested in building ties with educational institutions, researchers, young people and rural communities to expand opportunities for knowledge and experimentation.

We want those who work at San Juan to be able to improve their living conditions and those of their families.

And we want new generations to see the countryside as a place where it is worth developing their talent.

Our dream is not that people have to work in the countryside. It is that they want to.

15. Abundance must create value for everyone

We believe a farm can be worth far more for what it is able to produce sustainably than for what it represents as a mere stretch of land.

Location, access and the real estate market all influence a property’s value. But its capacity to generate income, profit and cash flow over time should matter too.

We want to build a company that improves that productive capacity and turns what it learns into economic value.

We aren’t interested in producing more at any cost. We are interested in producing better, using capital efficiently and building an operation that can sustain its results.

How we want to do it at San Juan

We want to progressively improve feed production, sustainable stocking rate, weight gain and economic productivity per hectare.

We will evaluate investments in infrastructure, technology and knowledge by their capacity to generate value.

We also want to explore new sources of income, commercial partnerships and business models that build on the capabilities we develop.

We will seek financing structures that let us build the operation with partners who share a long-term vision.

If we manage to prove a profitable, repeatable model, we aspire to take that knowledge to other properties and producers.

We want the value of Ganadería San Juan to grow because its real capacity to produce, learn and prosper grows.

How we want to turn our beliefs into reality

Our first big task: prove it on our own land

We don’t want to begin by telling other ranchers how to work.

We want to begin with ourselves.

San Juan de Frías and San Juan de Potosí will be the first places where we put our convictions to the test.

Our starting point will be to understand the state of each farm: its soils, pastures, water sources, infrastructure, animals, costs and productive capacity.

From that knowledge we want to design grazing systems, build infrastructure, train teams, bring in technologies and set up processes for measurement and continuous improvement.

We don’t imagine a transformation that happens overnight.

We want to move forward in stages, learn from every investment and develop an operation that can grow without losing its economic discipline or compromising its biological capacity.

A company that never stops learning

We want experimentation to be an everyday capability at Ganadería San Juan.

We imagine workers who ask questions, record observations, compare alternatives and propose improvements.

We want to develop relationships with universities, researchers, technology companies, producers and organizations that help us expand our knowledge.

We aspire for our farms to be places where science and practical experience meet, and where every lesson can become a better decision.

A company that finds new ways to create value

Our purpose doesn’t end at producing cattle.

We want to explore how the capabilities we develop can open new opportunities: better commercial relationships, services for other producers, research partnerships, education, applied technologies and other economically viable sources of income.

Not all of these possibilities will become businesses.

But we believe a truly innovative company must stay curious enough to discover opportunities it doesn’t yet know about.

A company that protects what must not be lost

Yuwú is part of this story.

Its forest reminds us that the value of land is not expressed only in what we can extract or sell.

We want to protect it, understand its biodiversity and find partners who will help conserve it over the long term.

Our aspiration is for the productivity of the cattle areas and the conservation of the forest to coexist within an economically responsible project.

A company worth building

We know this vision demands capital, knowledge, time, discipline and committed people.

We also know it involves risks, difficulties and decisions we cannot yet anticipate.

We don’t claim our convictions are enough to guarantee success.

We want them to be clear enough to guide our work, demanding enough to force us to improve, and open enough to evolve with what we learn.

We are looking for partners who want to help us build and prove this vision.

People and organizations who understand that transforming a productive activity doesn’t happen only by introducing new technologies or investing more capital, but by developing a different way of thinking, working and creating value.

What we want to prove

That we can improve the condition of our soils and increase their productive capacity.

That we can learn to be better herders and produce more feed and more protein per hectare.

That we can build a profitable company without giving up the protection of biodiversity.

That the oldest knowledge about nature can live alongside the most advanced technologies.

That creativity can also transform the financing, marketing and business models of ranching.

That people can grow, prosper and find happiness working in the countryside.

And that a family inheritance can become the starting point for something much bigger.

We don’t just want to have better farms.

We want to build a better way of running a cattle company.

Because we believe true abundance happens when the land produces, nature thrives, people grow and the value we create can last over time.

Ganadería San Juan. Honor our roots. Transform our future.
